* Overview
This guide covers the custom Calendar + Markdown-diary workflow provided by the
=timeline= module. The module lives in
[[file+emacs:/Users/jay/emacs/emacs-settings/timeline/][/Users/jay/emacs/emacs-settings/timeline/]] and is loaded from
[[file+emacs:/Users/jay/emacs/emacs-settings/shared-functions.org][📄 shared-functions.org]] via ~(require 'timeline)~.

The diary file is the plain-text [[file+emacs:/Users/jay/Dropbox/github/timeless/data/timeline.md][Timeless Markdown timeline]]. It doubles as the live data file for the *Timeless* app, so it can be edited from either Emacs or that app. See [[file+emacs:/Users/jay/emacs/emacs-settings/timeline/README.org][📄 timeline/README.org]] for the design philosophy and full feature
list, and [[file+emacs:/Users/jay/emacs/emacs-settings/timeline/timeline-keybindings.org][📄 timeline/timeline-keybindings.org]] for the complete binding
reference.

Opening Calendar saves your current window layout, then shows the previous, current, and next months side by side in a short strip above the full-width itinerary. Dates with diary entries use the mint-green ~diary~ face (~#6FCFA6~), and today is marked separately. Auto-revert relies on polling rather than cloud-storage file notifications, so external edits appear after a short delay.

* Quick Start

- Capture without leaving your work: ~s-k c c~, then the date and event text. ~s-k c s~ continues to schedule an Org item.
- Browse: ~M-x calendar~, then ~j~ and a date such as ~oct 12~. The itinerary follows automatically; ~V~ toggles following.
- Edit: ~RET~/~e~ opens the diary in the lower pane; ~C-c C-c~ returns to browsing.
- Exit: ~q~ from the grid restores the previous layout. An editor that is still active stays visible.

* Opening the Calendar
- ~M-x calendar~ --- Open the calendar (Spacemacs: ~SPC a d c~ unless remapped).
- ~q~ --- Restore the window layout you had before opening Calendar. If you are still editing the diary, quit closes the grid and keeps that editor visible.

* Navigation
Standard Emacs calendar motion still works (~C-f~/~f~/~→~ day forward,
~C-b~/~b~/~←~ day back, ~C-n~/~↓~ week down, ~C-p~/~↑~ week up, ~.~ to today,
~j~ or ~g d~ to jump with an Org date prompt). The =timeline= module adds these:

- ~j~, ~g d~ --- Enter ~fri~, ~oct 12~, or an explicit date. Org interprets ~+3~ relative to today and ~++3~ relative to the selected date; pressing Return accepts the selected date. The other ~g~ prefix commands remain available.
- ~C-c <left>~, ~C-c <right>~ --- Back and forward through deliberate jumps made with the natural-date prompt, today/edit, today toggle, or an itinerary/Upcoming entry. Arrow movement does not add history.
- ~V~ --- Toggle automatic itinerary following. The preference is saved through Emacs Custom; opening Calendar still shows an initial view of its selected date.
- ~n~, ~M-<right>~ (⌥→) --- Forward one month.
- ~p~, ~M-<left>~ (⌥←) --- Backward one month.
- ~N~ --- Forward one year.
- ~P~ --- Backward one year.
- ~t~ --- Jump to *today* and open today's entry for editing, creating it if
  needed, landing at the end of the last bullet.
- ~M-t~ --- Toggle between today and the previously visited date (like a
  one-slot mark ring).
- ~a~ --- =my-timeline-upcoming=. Open the read-only *Timeline Upcoming* buffer,
  grouped into Today, This week, and Later. Paired multi-day boundaries collapse
  into one range with status such as ~Starts today~, ~Ongoing · ends tomorrow~,
  or ~Last day today~. A numeric prefix caps the count (default 15); ~C-u a~
  shows all. In that buffer, ~RET~/~o~ jumps to the entry or range start in the
  Calendar, ~g~ refreshes, and ~q~ quits.
- ~?~ --- Pop up the custom cheat-sheet (press ~q~ to close).

The Monday-first week (~calendar-week-start-day 1~) treats the weekend as a unit.

* Working With Diary Entries From the Calendar
These custom bindings replace several stock calendar keys:

- ~RET~, ~e~ --- =my-calendar-edit-diary-entry=. Open the entry for the date at point in the lower pane, creating the date heading and a blank bullet if none exists, and land at the end of the last bullet ready to type. Following pauses until you return with ~C-c C-c~.
- ~v~, ~o~, ~SPC~ --- ~my-calendar-view-diary-entry~. Show the itinerary for the selected day while keeping focus in Calendar, including when following is off. An active diary editor takes precedence; return with ~C-c C-c~ to resume browsing.
- ~O~ --- =my-calendar-show-fancy-diary-listing=. Show the stock
  ~*Fancy Diary Entries*~ listing for the date, then reselect the Calendar and
  jump to the Markdown entry.
- ~i~, ~c~ --- =my-calendar-insert-diary-entry=. Prompt in the minibuffer for
  entry text and insert it chronologically. With a prefix arg (~C-u i~) focus
  stays in the diary buffer afterward.
- ~C~ --- =my-calendar-insert-diary-entry-and-autopopulate=. Same, but seeds the
  minibuffer with your most recent entry text for quick reuse.
- ~I~ --- =diary-insert-entry=. The stock Emacs single-day inserter, kept as a
  Markdown-agnostic fallback.
- ~/~, ~s~ --- =my-diary-search=. Search the whole diary via ~consult-ripgrep~.

Note: because ~i~ is remapped to the custom inserter, the stock ~i d~ / ~i w~ /
~i m~ / ~i y~ / ~i a~ / ~i b~ / ~i c~ recurring-entry sub-bindings are *not*
available from the calendar in this setup. See the appendix for what those
stock types mean if you ever want to add a sexp entry manually.

* Following Itinerary

The lower pane is a read-only Org view covering one week before and three weeks after the selected date. Earlier, selected, and later events appear together. Paired multi-day events show their full dates and appear in the selected section while ongoing, even on days with no separate diary entry. An empty selected day offers ~i~ to add an entry and shows the next event with an absolute date.

- ~RET~, ~e~ --- Edit the event at point, or its range's first-day description, in the same lower pane.
- ~i~ --- Add an entry to the date at point; on an empty selected day, use that selected date.
- ~<~, ~>~ --- Extend the displayed range by four weeks earlier or later. ~Page Up~/~Page Down~ and ~M-v~/~C-v~ extend it when scrolling reaches the beginning or end.
- ~g~ --- Refresh from the live diary buffer, including unsaved changes.
- ~q~ --- Return focus to the grid; ~q~ in the grid exits the calendar session.

Calendar movement leaves the source diary's point and text alone. Editing pauses following, including if you focus the grid with ~s-.~ and move to another date. ~C-c C-c~ returns to the date you opened and swaps the editor back to the itinerary. Upcoming and the itinerary share their Org setup and disable Olivetti locally so its writing margins do not narrow the pane.

* Capture Without Opening Calendar

Press ~s-k c c~ (or run ~M-x my-timeline-capture~) from any Emacs buffer. Enter a natural-language date and the event text; capture inserts it chronologically and returns to your current work. A previously clean diary is saved. If the diary already contains unsaved edits, capture leaves those edits and the new entry unsaved rather than saving other work implicitly.

* Inside the Diary Buffer (timeline.md)
When ~timeline.md~ is open in ~markdown-mode~, =my-diary-mode= activates
automatically and shows a 📅 plus the current date in the mode line. These
buffer-local keys are installed:

- ~RET~ --- The existing ~my-markdown-newline-with-bullet~ continues a populated bullet; Return on an empty bullet ends the list entry.
- ~s-.~ (⌘.) --- =my-calendar-focus-calendar-window=. Refocus the live Calendar
  window (and close stray fancy-diary panes) without changing its date.
- ~C-c C-c~ --- =my-diary-return-to-calendar=. Jump back to the Calendar at the
  date you originally opened, even after editing elsewhere. (~C-SPC~ is left as
  the stock ~set-mark-command~ so region selection works normally.)
- ~C-c C-k~ --- =my-calendar-cancel-current-entry=. Delete the current date
  block (briefly highlighted first). If the block has content it asks for
  confirmation; then it pops back to the Calendar.
- ~/~ --- =my-diary-search= (the stock ~s~ self-insert is preserved for typing).

*Auto-cleanup:* saving the diary removes empty date stubs (a date heading whose
only bullet is blank), so exploring or cancelling entries never leaves debris.

* Diary File Format
Entries are grouped by year and month with Markdown headings, then a bare
~M/D/YYYY~ date line (no leading zeros) followed by two-space bullets:

#+begin_example
# 2025

## January 2025

1/5/2025
  - Meet with Claire
  - A completed item [✓] #tag
#+end_example

- ~# YYYY~ and ~## Month YYYY~ headings are treated as diary *comments*
  (~diary-comment-start~ is ~#~), so Emacs' diary parser ignores them while the
  file still reads as clean Markdown.
- A trailing ~[✓]~ marks a completed item; ~#tags~ are supported (see
  =/Users/jay/Dropbox/github/timeless/docs/sample-diary.md=).
- New entries are inserted into the correct year/month section in date order,
  creating missing headings as needed.

** Multi-Day Events
The canonical shared-data protocol lives in [[file+emacs:/Users/jay/Dropbox/github/timeless/docs/instructions.org][Timeless Instructions]]. Record the full description and complete date range on the first day, add ~Last day of [event]~ on the final day, and do not repeat the event on intervening days. The upcoming view pairs those two boundaries for display, keeps active ranges visible after their start date, and leaves the Markdown representation unchanged. Do not use ~%%(diary-block ...)~ because Timeless and the custom agenda do not parse it.

* Configuration Reference
Defined in [[file+emacs:/Users/jay/emacs/emacs-settings/timeline/timeline-core.el][timeline-core.el]] and applied by [[file+emacs:/Users/jay/emacs/emacs-settings/timeline/timeline-calendar.el][timeline-calendar.el]]:

- ~my-timeline-diary-file~ defaults to [[file+emacs:/Users/jay/Dropbox/github/timeless/data/timeline.md][/Users/jay/Dropbox/github/timeless/data/timeline.md]] and keeps Emacs' stock ~diary-file~ synchronized. Change it with ~M-x customize-option RET my-timeline-diary-file~ or set it before ~(require 'timeline)~.
- ~calendar-week-start-day~ = 1 (Monday)
- ~calendar-mark-holidays-flag~ = nil, ~calendar-holidays~ = nil (holidays off)
- ~calendar-mark-diary-entries-flag~ = t (diary dates highlighted)
- ~calendar-view-diary-initially-flag~ = nil (no automatic stock diary listing; the custom itinerary still opens)
- ~my-timeline-follow-preview~ defaults to ~t~ and is saved through Custom when toggled with ~V~.
- ~calendar-mark-today~ runs from ~calendar-today-visible-hook~ alongside the existing Org-roam daily-entry marker.
- ~diary-display-function~ = ~diary-fancy-display~
- ~diary-comment-start~ = ~#~
- The ~diary~ face is restyled to mint-green ~#6FCFA6~; the ~holiday~ face is
  neutralized to render like a normal day.

* Implementation Contracts

These are the implemented decisions from the September 8, 2026 calendar work, accepted in this session.

- The Markdown diary remains the shared source. Org headings and ~-~ list syntax belong to the generated views. Paired ranges follow [[file+emacs:/Users/jay/Dropbox/github/timeless/docs/instructions.org][the Timeless first-day/last-day protocol]]; the views reuse the existing collectors rather than maintaining another parser.
- Browsing preserves the source buffer's text, modified state, point, and narrowing. ~my-timeline--data~ caches entries and ranges in the source buffer using ~buffer-chars-modified-tick~; unsaved character edits and text loaded by auto-revert invalidate the cache. A refresh reads the live buffer. It does not force a disk revert.
- Upcoming, itinerary, and help reuse ~my-timeline--view-buffer~. Their Org mode initializes once; refreshes replace generated text in the same read-only buffer. The helper disables Olivetti locally and sets ~disable-olivetti-auto-toggle~ so the existing split-window hook does not restore writing margins.
- The frame's ~my-timeline-session~ parameter holds the original window configuration, grid window, lower window, and editing state. The lower pane shows either the itinerary or the source editor. Automatic and manual preview both leave an active editor in place. ~C-c C-c~ ends that pause. Browsing quit restores the saved layout; editing quit closes the grid and preserves the editor.
- Explicit calendar openings get the stacked view. Internal calendar calls and date prompts retain their existing behavior. ~org-read-date~ prompts use the selected date as the default without opening an extra calendar.
- Diary shortcuts belong to ~my-diary-mode-map~. Its ~s-.~ override uses a buffer-local ~minor-mode-overriding-map-alist~ entry to take precedence over ~key-minor-mode-map~. Global capture belongs to ~my/global-key-bindings~ in [[file+emacs:/Users/jay/emacs/emacs-settings/keys.el][keys.el]]. The itinerary uses ~<~/~>~ for extension because the live Spacemacs emulation map owns ~]~.
- The diary lighter evaluates the date block at point. Calendar face restyling uses ~enable-theme-functions~ and ~disable-theme-functions~. The Calendar ~?~ guide resolves displayed calendar/diary keys from their maps; its capture section also displays the global capture shortcut when available.
- [[file+emacs:/Users/jay/emacs/emacs-settings/timeline/timeline-README.org][timeline-README.org]] is a symlink to [[file+emacs:/Users/jay/emacs/emacs-settings/timeline/README.org][README.org]]. The implementation is in the module's Elisp files; it has no separate literate source to tangle.

The existing test suite can be run from [[file+emacs:/Users/jay/emacs/emacs-settings/][the configuration repository]] with ~emacs --batch -Q -L timeline -l timeline --eval '(ert-run-tests-batch-and-exit "^my-\\(calendar\\|timeline\\)-test-")'~. It passed 23 tests for the September 8 implementation. Live verification also covered keymap precedence, the stacked window sizes and margins, the today marker, paused editing, and layout restoration.

References: [[https://orgmode.org/manual/The-date_002ftime-prompt.html][Org's date prompt]] describes relative-date semantics. The installed Emacs documentation for ~current-window-configuration~, ~buffer-chars-modified-tick~, and ~minor-mode-overriding-map-alist~ describes the underlying APIs; [[https://www.gnu.org/software/emacs/manual/html_node/elisp/Active-Keymaps.html][the GNU keymap reference]] specifies precedence. The separate [[https://emacs.stackexchange.com/questions/352/how-to-override-major-mode-bindings][practitioner discussion of keymap overrides]] provides examples; the Timeline-specific bindings and Spacemacs conflict above were checked in the live session.

* Workflow Examples
** Daily review
1. ~M-x calendar~.
2. ~t~ --- jump to today and land in today's entry ready to type.
3. Add bullets, then ~C-c C-c~ to hop back to the Calendar (or ~s-.~ to just
   refocus it).

** Weekly planning
1. ~M-x calendar~, then ~.~ to today.
2. Move through the week with ~C-f~/~C-b~ (or down a week with ~C-n~).
3. Read the following itinerary as you move. If following is off, ~v~/~SPC~ shows the selected date manually; ~e~ opens its editor.

** Finding something
- ~/~ or ~s~ from the Calendar (or ~/~ inside the diary) runs
  ~consult-ripgrep~ across the timeline --- e.g. search "flight" or "birthday".

* Troubleshooting
- *"Could not find entry for date"* --- the exact ~M/D/YYYY~ line isn't in the
  file. Press ~e~/~RET~ instead to create it. (The lookup is now anchored to the
  start of a line, so it won't accidentally match a date mentioned inside a
  bullet.)
- *Itinerary not following* --- ~V~ controls automatic following. If the lower pane is editing the diary, use ~C-c C-c~ to resume the itinerary; focusing the grid with ~s-.~ does not end the editing pause.
- *External edits not visible yet* --- auto-revert polls the shared diary. After it has loaded the external changes, ~g~ in the itinerary refreshes from that buffer. Unsaved buffer edits are included as well; a preview refresh does not discard them by reverting from disk.
- *Custom keys not active* --- ensure ~(require 'timeline)~ has loaded; the
  bindings install via ~(with-eval-after-load 'calendar ...)~ in
  [[file+emacs:/Users/jay/emacs/emacs-settings/timeline/timeline.el][timeline.el]]. Restart Emacs if needed.

* Appendix: Stock Emacs Diary Entry Types
These stock-diary examples are reference material for a separate Emacs diary, not supported entries for the shared Timeless Markdown file. The custom Timeline parser does not expand diary sexps. Use the shared format described above for entries that must appear in both Timeline and Timeless.

- ~%%(diary-weekly)~ --- recurs on a given weekday.
- ~%%(diary-monthly) DAY~ --- recurs on a day of the month.
- ~%%(diary-yearly) M/D~ --- recurs on a month/day each year.
- ~%%(diary-anniversary M D Y) text~ --- yearly, and shows years elapsed.
- ~%%(diary-block M D Y M D Y) text~ --- spans a continuous date range.
- ~%%(diary-cyclic N M D Y) text~ --- recurs every N days from a start date.
