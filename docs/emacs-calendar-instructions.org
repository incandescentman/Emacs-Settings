* Overview
This guide covers the custom Calendar + Markdown-diary workflow provided by the
=timeline= module. The module lives in
[[file+emacs:/Users/jay/emacs/emacs-settings/timeline/][/Users/jay/emacs/emacs-settings/timeline/]] and is loaded from
[[file+emacs:/Users/jay/emacs/emacs-settings/shared-functions.org][📄 shared-functions.org]] via ~(require 'timeline)~.

The diary file is a plain-text Markdown timeline at
=/Users/jay/Dropbox/github/timeless/data/timeline.md=. It doubles as the live
data file for the *Timeless* app, so it can be edited from either Emacs or that
app. See [[file+emacs:/Users/jay/emacs/emacs-settings/timeline/README.org][📄 timeline/README.org]] for the design philosophy and full feature
list, and [[file+emacs:/Users/jay/emacs/emacs-settings/timeline/timeline-keybindings.org][📄 timeline/timeline-keybindings.org]] for the complete binding
reference.

When you launch the Calendar, the window shows the current month centered, with
the previous and next months above/below. Dates that have diary entries are
highlighted with the ~diary~ face, which this config restyles to a mint-green
foreground (~#6FCFA6~). Auto-revert relies on polling (file notifications are
disabled for cloud storage), so external edits appear after a short delay.

* Opening the Calendar
- ~M-x calendar~ --- Open the calendar (Spacemacs: ~SPC a d c~ unless remapped).
- ~q~ --- Quit the calendar (bury buffer).

* Navigation
Standard Emacs calendar motion still works (~C-f~/~f~/~→~ day forward,
~C-b~/~b~/~←~ day back, ~C-n~/~↓~ week down, ~C-p~/~↑~ week up, ~.~ to today,
~g d~ to jump to a specific date). The =timeline= module adds these:

- ~n~, ~M-<right>~ (⌥→) --- Forward one month.
- ~p~, ~M-<left>~ (⌥←) --- Backward one month.
- ~N~ --- Forward one year.
- ~P~ --- Backward one year.
- ~t~ --- Jump to *today* and open today's entry for editing, creating it if
  needed, landing at the end of the last bullet.
- ~M-t~ --- Toggle between today and the previously visited date (like a
  one-slot mark ring).
- ~a~ --- =my-timeline-upcoming=. Open the read-only *Timeline Upcoming* buffer:
  future-dated entries in chronological order with weekday and days-until. A
  numeric prefix caps the count (default 15); ~C-u a~ shows all. In that buffer,
  ~RET~/~o~ jumps to the date in the Calendar, ~g~ refreshes, ~q~ quits.
- ~?~ --- Pop up the custom cheat-sheet (press ~q~ to close).

The Monday-first week (~calendar-week-start-day 1~) treats the weekend as a unit.

* Working With Diary Entries From the Calendar
These custom bindings replace several stock calendar keys:

- ~RET~, ~e~ --- =my-calendar-edit-diary-entry=. Open the entry for the date at
  point in a side window, creating the date heading and a blank bullet if none
  exists, and land at the end of the last bullet ready to type. Focus stays in
  the diary buffer.
- ~v~, ~o~, ~SPC~ --- =my-calendar-view-diary-entry=. Reveal the entry in the
  side window but keep focus in the Calendar.
- ~O~ --- =my-calendar-show-fancy-diary-listing=. Show the stock
  ~*Fancy Diary Entries*~ listing for the date, then reselect the Calendar and
  jump to the Markdown entry.
- ~i~, ~c~ --- =my-calendar-insert-diary-entry=. Prompt in the minibuffer for
  entry text and insert it chronologically. With a prefix arg (~C-u i~) focus
  stays in the diary buffer afterward.
- ~C~ --- =my-calendar-insert-diary-entry-and-autopopulate=. Same, but seeds the
  minibuffer with your most recent entry text for quick reuse.
- ~I~ --- =diary-insert-entry=. The stock Emacs single-day inserter, kept as a
  Markdown-agnostic fallback.
- ~/~, ~s~ --- =my-diary-search=. Search the whole diary via ~consult-ripgrep~.

Note: because ~i~ is remapped to the custom inserter, the stock ~i d~ / ~i w~ /
~i m~ / ~i y~ / ~i a~ / ~i b~ / ~i c~ recurring-entry sub-bindings are *not*
available from the calendar in this setup. See the appendix for what those
stock types mean if you ever want to add a sexp entry manually.

* Inside the Diary Buffer (timeline.md)
When ~timeline.md~ is open in ~markdown-mode~, =my-diary-mode= activates
automatically and shows a 📅 plus the current date in the mode line. These
buffer-local keys are installed:

- ~s-.~ (⌘.) --- =my-calendar-focus-calendar-window=. Refocus the live Calendar
  window (and close stray fancy-diary panes) without changing its date.
- ~C-c C-c~ --- =my-diary-return-to-calendar=. Jump back to the Calendar at the
  date you originally opened, even after editing elsewhere. (~C-SPC~ is left as
  the stock ~set-mark-command~ so region selection works normally.)
- ~C-c C-k~ --- =my-calendar-cancel-current-entry=. Delete the current date
  block (briefly highlighted first). If the block has content it asks for
  confirmation; then it pops back to the Calendar.
- ~/~ --- =my-diary-search= (the stock ~s~ self-insert is preserved for typing).

*Auto-cleanup:* saving the diary removes empty date stubs (a date heading whose
only bullet is blank), so exploring or cancelling entries never leaves debris.

* Diary File Format
Entries are grouped by year and month with Markdown headings, then a bare
~M/D/YYYY~ date line (no leading zeros) followed by two-space bullets:

#+begin_example
# 2025

## January 2025

1/5/2025
  - Meet with Claire
  - A completed item [✓] #tag
#+end_example

- ~# YYYY~ and ~## Month YYYY~ headings are treated as diary *comments*
  (~diary-comment-start~ is ~#~), so Emacs' diary parser ignores them while the
  file still reads as clean Markdown.
- A trailing ~[✓]~ marks a completed item; ~#tags~ are supported (see
  =/Users/jay/Dropbox/github/timeless/docs/sample-diary.md=).
- New entries are inserted into the correct year/month section in date order,
  creating missing headings as needed.

** Multi-Day Events
The canonical shared-data protocol lives in [[file+emacs:/Users/jay/Dropbox/github/timeless/docs/instructions.org][Timeless Instructions]]. Record the full description and complete date range on the first day, add ~Last day of [event]~ on the final day, and do not repeat the event on intervening days. Do not use ~%%(diary-block ...)~ because Timeless and the custom agenda do not parse it.

* Workflow Examples
** Daily review
1. ~M-x calendar~.
2. ~t~ --- jump to today and land in today's entry ready to type.
3. Add bullets, then ~C-c C-c~ to hop back to the Calendar (or ~s-.~ to just
   refocus it).

** Weekly planning
1. ~M-x calendar~, then ~.~ to today.
2. Move through the week with ~C-f~/~C-b~ (or down a week with ~C-n~).
3. ~v~ / ~SPC~ to preview each day's entry without leaving the Calendar; ~e~ to
   edit one.

** Finding something
- ~/~ or ~s~ from the Calendar (or ~/~ inside the diary) runs
  ~consult-ripgrep~ across the timeline --- e.g. search "flight" or "birthday".

* Troubleshooting
- *"Could not find entry for date"* --- the exact ~M/D/YYYY~ line isn't in the
  file. Press ~e~/~RET~ instead to create it. (The lookup is now anchored to the
  start of a line, so it won't accidentally match a date mentioned inside a
  bullet.)
- *Calendar not updating after edits* --- auto-revert is poll-based for cloud
  storage; wait a few seconds or run ~M-x revert-buffer~ in ~timeline.md~. Also
  remember the Timeless app may be editing the same file.
- *Custom keys not active* --- ensure ~(require 'timeline)~ has loaded; the
  bindings install via ~(with-eval-after-load 'calendar ...)~ in
  [[file+emacs:/Users/jay/emacs/emacs-settings/timeline/timeline.el][timeline.el]]. Restart Emacs if needed.

* Appendix: Stock Emacs Diary Entry Types
These are standard Emacs diary sexp entries. They are *not* wired to ~i~ in this
Markdown workflow (which uses the custom inserter), but you can add them by hand
if you ever want recurrence. They live as non-comment lines in the diary file.

- ~%%(diary-weekly)~ --- recurs on a given weekday.
- ~%%(diary-monthly) DAY~ --- recurs on a day of the month.
- ~%%(diary-yearly) M/D~ --- recurs on a month/day each year.
- ~%%(diary-anniversary M D Y) text~ --- yearly, and shows years elapsed.
- ~%%(diary-block M D Y M D Y) text~ --- spans a continuous date range.
- ~%%(diary-cyclic N M D Y) text~ --- recurs every N days from a start date.
